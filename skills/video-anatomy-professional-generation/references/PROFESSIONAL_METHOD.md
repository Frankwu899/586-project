# Shared professional method

Apply these lenses selectively. Their purpose is sound decisions, not an exhaustive
visible checklist. Reuse supplied answers and accepted context.

## 1. Understand the job
Separate the business aspiration, the communication response and the artifact
requested now. A piece can introduce, explain, build desire, establish an association,
answer an objection or invite action; do not default to conversion.
For Structure, infer the source video's job with evidence. For Ideas and Storyboard,
preserve the user's chosen job. Distinguish creator intent from observable execution
and possible audience response.

## 2. Inventory evidence and constraints
Identify the exact sources, revisions, measured source timing, modality coverage,
approved product facts, wording, language and binding production limits.
Separate what is shown, said, claimed, researched and inferred. A demonstration,
testimonial and independent result supply different kinds of support.
Missing evidence is different from evidence of absence. A missing field is not
permission to invent a convenient fact.

## 3. Investigate material context
Resolve unfamiliar product, company, category, campaign or cultural context when
it could change the diagnosis. Follow RESEARCH.md. Use relevant available tools
proactively; do not ask the user to retrieve a public fact the host can establish.
For Structure, show the initial video-based reading and research status first.
A company positioning statement informs interpretation; it does not establish
this video's intended audience, message or success.

## 4. Form and challenge the diagnosis
Consider the relationships below; choose those that explain this task:
- **Audience situation:** motivation, prior knowledge, tension, objection and
  viewing context. A demographic label alone is not an insight.
- **Value and support:** feature → useful benefit → audience relevance → available
  support. Keep source advertising claims attributed.
- **Communication:** attention, comprehension, emotion, belief, memory and action.
  Explain how a specific creative choice may affect one of these.
- **Brand and medium:** product role, recognizable brand cues, tone, format,
  cultural meaning, sound dependence and pace.
- **Execution:** resources, continuity, reading/speech/action time and constraints.
- **Alternatives:** counterevidence and other defensible explanations. Do not
  manufacture alternatives or present a speculative primary interpretation as fact.

Ask useful diagnostic questions internally: does the opening earn a payoff?
Would removing the product weaken the idea? Does the scene explain a benefit or
merely borrow an association? Could the audience understand it without sound?
Use brand-swap and product-removal checks as diagnostics, not universal rejection
rules. An awareness-led or experimental concept can be useful without a demo.

## 5. Decide and develop
Select the supported insights that change understanding or action. Build the
requested artifact using the module guide. Preserve ambition while making the
assumptions, tradeoffs and execution conditions assessable.
For each major point, connect the choice to its proposed mechanism and evidence.
Avoid labels such as "authentic" or "engaging" without that explanation.

## 6. Ask at a consequential fork
Ask when an unresolved user choice changes the goal, approved message, material
constraint or factual claim. Research lookup questions; infer low-risk creative
details with visible assumptions. Never infer missing product facts.
Ask one focused question by default; batch up to three only when they are independent.
Explain the decision it affects, offer concrete alternatives and allow free text.
A recommendation may explain a tradeoff but must not make the choice for the user.
Use interactive controls when available. Do not require a full interview before
delivering useful supported work or repeat confirmed decisions.

Example: "Is the main barrier understanding the benefit or believing it?
That changes whether the concept should emphasize explanation or proof."
Ask only if the brief, evidence and accessible context leave this unresolved.

If a critical choice blocks one part, identify it and continue the unaffected work.
In a host without interactive replies, return a concise needs-decision outcome;
do not pretend the user answered.

## 7. Communicate and check
Apply EDITORIAL.md, then QUALITY.md. Identify the cause of a weakness before
repairing it: missing extraction, conflicting brief, weak mechanism, generic idea,
timing, provider failure and poor presentation need different remedies.
A fluent answer is not proof of grounding; "bad model" is not a diagnosis.
