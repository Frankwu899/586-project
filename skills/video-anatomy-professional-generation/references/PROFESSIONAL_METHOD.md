# Shared professional method

These are operational lenses, not a compulsory essay outline. Apply the relevant
ones and leave unsupported dimensions unknown. Strategy must fit the task; no
single advertising theory or formula is universally correct.

## 1. Define the communication job

Distinguish the business aspiration from the response this piece can plausibly
influence. Awareness, understanding, trust, preference, participation and purchase
are different jobs. Identify the requested response, audience situation, channel,
duration and non-negotiable constraints. Do not default every brief to conversion.
In source analysis, the creator's intended job is a hypothesis unless documented.
If several supported explanations exist, present them in parallel without ranking
or claiming an official priority.

## 2. Understand people in a situation

Consider prior knowledge, attention state, practical and emotional needs,
objections, social context, cultural familiarity and participation costs. A useful
insight connects a situation to a motivation or tension; a demographic label alone
is not an insight. Do not infer that every middle-aged man likes Guandan or that
all beginners prioritize the lowest price. Identify the support and boundary of
an audience hypothesis. Distinguish the person attracted by a hook from the
person likely to value the product.

Example decision question, when truly needed: “Should this direction help a
first-time buyer understand the benefit, or help an experienced buyer evaluate
the configuration?” Do not ask for that information if it is already in the brief.

## 3. Build the value argument

Separate four levels: product feature; usable benefit in a situation; reason that
benefit matters to this audience; credible support. Identify the strongest
supported proposition, meaningful alternatives and unaddressed objections.
An advertised claim, an on-camera demonstration and an independent result are
not interchangeable types of proof. Never turn product appearance into performance
proof, configuration into an invented discount, or proximity into endorsement.
A borrowed reference mechanism cannot supply missing facts about the new product.

## 4. Explain how communication works

Inspect the relationship between attention, comprehension, relevance, belief,
emotion, memory and action. These are distinct mechanisms, not mandatory stages.
A surprising opening can attract attention without establishing product value;
a credible demonstration can explain a benefit without proving an actual sales
lift. Identify what the viewer sees/hears, the interpretation it may invite,
why that interpretation could matter, and what remains unproven.

Useful checks: does the opening earn its payoff? Does evidence arrive when a
viewer needs it? Is the product causally relevant to the scene? Does the invitation
to act connect to what preceded it? Can the viewer understand the claim without
missing audio or small text? An inference must state its bridge, not merely use
words such as “engagement,” “authenticity,” or “emotional connection.”

## 5. Develop and compare creative hypotheses

Use a working proposition: “For this audience in this situation, this execution
may make this value meaningful through this mechanism.” Then consider alternatives
that change the central idea, persuasion mechanism or narrative perspective.
Compare task fit, evidence, brand fit, distinctiveness and execution demands.
Do not equate novelty with relevance or familiar structure with poor creativity.

Use counterfactual checks as diagnostics, not absolute rejection rules:
- **Brand swap:** if any brand could be substituted, is that genericity intentional
  for this objective or evidence of a weak connection?
- **Product removal:** if the product disappears, does the scene still communicate
  the intended value? If so, what connects the memory back to the brand?
- **Audience change:** would the same explanation apply to everyone? What situation
  or barrier makes it specifically useful here?
- **Evidence removal:** does the claim still sound persuasive only because prose
  hides an unsupported assumption?

An attention-led idea with an uncertain brand connection may remain experimental.
State its goal hypothesis and validation method; do not pretend it is already
successful or eliminate it solely for lacking a direct product demonstration.

## 6. Fit brand, medium and execution

Check tone, brand associations, differentiation, viewing format, pacing, readable
text, sound dependence and production requirements. Mark assumed resources as
required, not already available. For Storyboard, design the ideal realization
first unless the user has supplied binding production limits; list resources,
then propose compression options when actual conditions become known. Do not
silently impose a low-budget aesthetic or ignore an explicit resource restriction.

## 7. Diagnose and learn

Keep causes separate: missing source coverage, contradictory brief, unsupported
claim, weak mechanism, generic method, near-duplicate concepts, timing failure,
continuity failure, provider failure and poor interface feedback. “Bad model”
and “needs more training” are hypotheses, not diagnoses.

Check observable relationships and professional usefulness, not just fluent prose.
Record the minimal rationale and unresolved assumptions. Where relevant, state
what evidence would weaken the claim or what a small concept test could examine.
Do not invent benchmarks, confidence percentages, test participants or results.

## Asking like a professional

Ask at the point of a real choice. Explain the effect of the answer. Prefer one
material tradeoff over a broad intake form. Independently research external facts
through authorized tools when that capability is available; report unavailable
research rather than inventing it. Research is bounded to relevant context.

If a user asks for jargon-heavy copy aimed at beginners, explain the comprehension
tradeoff and offer an alternative. The user may retain the request. A strategic
preference is not an approval gate; missing factual support still limits what can
be asserted. When target duration requires dropping a core approved message,
request confirmation before changing it.
