# Ideas — develop a creative proposition

Use the saved brief, approved product facts/wording, objective, generation language,
binding constraints and any selected Structure handoff. A reference is optional.

## Professional procedure
1. Frame the desired audience response and the situation, motivation or obstacle
   the work should address. Distinguish a useful tension from a demographic label.
2. Build the value argument: approved feature/benefit, why it matters and the
   available support. Missing product facts cannot become creative assumptions.
3. Investigate only context that could change the proposition; apply RESEARCH.md.
   Keep discovered product claims awaiting confirmation. If audience/goal context
   is incomplete but product facts suffice, proceed under explicit assumptions
   and explain what would change if those assumptions are wrong.
4. Develop propositions that differ in central idea, mechanism or narrative
   perspective. Explore contrast, demonstration, story, association, participation
   or other appropriate approaches without mechanically applying a list.
5. Adapt a selected reference mechanism only when its conditions fit. Explain an
   unsuitable method and offer another way to pursue the user's communication goal.
6. Challenge the concept: does the opening earn its payoff, is the product/brand
   connection meaningful, does it respect facts and task, and is the proposed
   realization assessable? Preserve creative ambition and state resource needs.
7. Repair identified weaknesses at most once, then retain qualifying directions.
   One is acceptable; for none, identify the specific gap through the supported
   needs-information/failure outcome. Do not pad a quota.

## Main result per direction
Lead with a specific title and one-sentence proposition. Then present:
- a concrete opening example;
- a short narrative progression showing the turn and payoff;
- the product/brand's role in that progression;
- a concise explanation of why the mechanism fits the audience and goal;
- material execution conditions, assumptions and tradeoffs.

Combine related information to keep the proposal easy to compare. Do not produce
full shot-by-shot execution before selection or substitute slogans for concepts.
Keep source links and supporting research accessible separately. Use the supported
contract; these content requirements are not new field names.

## Judgment and questions
Challenge a strategic contradiction with its consequence and alternatives.
The user may retain the request; do not substitute a different objective.
Ask when a consequential choice cannot be safely inferred, researched or deferred.
Deliver useful unaffected work while waiting. Preserve approved wording and
source-language quotations. A new hook cannot introduce an unapproved product promise.

Reference evidence and new creative audio have different roles. A visual-only
reference leaves its speech/music/SFX unknown; it does not require a silent new
film. Propose narration or music as new creative choices when useful, label
them accordingly, and obey explicit silence, no-narration or voice-over requests.
Use faithful audience language for ordinary confirmed facts. Retain fixed
approved statements and their necessary qualifications. Do not put internal
approval/process phrases such as "Our confirmed product scope is" in copy.

## Experiments and approval
An unusual concept with uncertain product linkage may remain experimental.
Carry an explicit label, goal hypothesis and proposed validation method; do not
invent thresholds, participants, results or a mandatory concept-test gate.
Experimental effectiveness is different from an unsupported factual claim.
Only the user approves an exact saved direction revision. Preserve drafts/history;
a modified direction does not inherit approval from an unchanged ID.
Explicitly approved experimental directions may proceed to Storyboard with their
status, hypothesis, validation points and fixed wording intact.

## Avoid
Two paraphrases presented as alternatives; fixed faithful/reorganized A/B routes;
different hooks masking the same idea; borrowed product benefits; stereotyped
audiences; generic emotional language; invented evidence for reference-free Ideas.
