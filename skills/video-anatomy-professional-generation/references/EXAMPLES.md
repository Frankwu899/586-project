# Calibration examples and counterexamples

These are **illustrative editorial examples**, not new video inspections, measured
model outputs or independently verified case studies. Owner-discussed Xiaomi and
rod details are conditional on the actual evidence supplied at runtime. No real
event ID or timestamp is invented here. Other products below are fictional.

## E1 — Guandan: explaining a mechanism

Weak: “Guandan is popular with middle-aged men, so the video definitely sells
cars to that demographic.”

Better, if actual visual evidence supports it: “A recognizable shared activity
makes the interior's usable space and table part of an ordinary situation rather
than an isolated specification claim. This may help viewers imagine the vehicle
supporting social time. Product demonstration and presenter approachability are
both plausible interpretations; neither is established as the creator's primary
intent. Audience overlap needs separate support, and effectiveness is unmeasured.”

Why: connects execution to interpretation, marks uncertainty and does not rank
parallel readings. Specific seat rotation must not be claimed from an ending
position or supplied background alone.

## E2 — Missing visuals

Input condition: only the utterance “this car is suitable for Guandan” is available.

Reject: “The rotating seats and large table visibly prove spaciousness.”
Accept: attribute the utterance; flag missing visual coverage. Present a spatial
use-case interpretation only as a hypothesis to verify and omit any method card
whose crucial demonstration premise is unsupported. Do not fill missing video
observations with a product webpage.

## E3 — Rod: feature-to-value translation

Weak: “Use an expert to show that customers get two rods for one price.”

Better, when supported: “Explain how interchangeable tips change usable setups,
then relate that flexibility to a plausible value argument. Transfer the
feature → use-case → value translation, not the literal fishing setting.”

Conditions: the new product must actually have distinct usable configurations;
value depends on the buyer needing them. A speaker's economy claim stays
attributed. Two tips do not establish two complete rods, a price advantage,
expert credentials or a tested performance benefit.

## E4 — A transferable method card

Title: Make a product benefit legible through a familiar activity.
Original choice: a supported scene places a product feature inside a recognizable
activity rather than explaining it only through specifications.
Mechanism: familiarity supplies a practical comparison point, potentially making
the benefit easier to imagine. This is an interpretation, not measured impact.
Conditions: activity relevant to the intended situation; benefit visibly and
truthfully supported; product essential enough to explain its role.
Do not copy: original celebrity, cultural activity, demographic claim or exact set.
Counterexample: a product without that feature or an audience unfamiliar with the
activity can make the scene misleading or obscure.
Evidence: attach actual supplied nodes at runtime, not this editorial example.

## E5 — Distinct Ideas, not different wording

Fictional brief: a lamp has an approved adjustable-head feature; no claims about
medical benefit or energy savings are supplied.

Near-duplicates: “A brighter desk,” and “Light up your desk,” both showing the
same feature demonstration and payoff.

More meaningfully different: a problem/solution scene about directing light to a
specific task; versus a design-led transformation showing one approved feature
across different desk arrangements. Each still needs an opening, narrative beats,
mechanism, task rationale and execution assumptions. Neither may invent health
benefits, battery life or comparative savings.

## E6 — Experimental Idea

A fictional humorous scene may get attention while brand linkage is uncertain.
Keep it labeled experimental. Hypothesis: viewers connect the scene's payoff to
the intended product association. Proposed validation: show a rough concept to
appropriate viewers and examine unaided brand/benefit association and
misinterpretations. This is a test proposal, not a completed study or universal
metric. Explicit approval may take it into Storyboard; retain the label there.

## E7 — Compact storyboard with linked notes

Fictional 6-second excerpt for the adjustable lamp; illustrative only, not a full
approved production result. Dialogue is absent, so no speech-timing claim is made.

| Shot | Time | Visual | Audio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | 00:00–00:03 | On a desk, a hand adjusts the lamp head toward an open notebook. Screen text: none. | Quiet room tone and adjustment sound. |
| 02 | 00:03–00:06 | Hold on the resulting arrangement; the same hand begins writing. Screen text: approved product name, if supplied. | Room tone continues; no voice-over. |

Separate notes: beat = demonstrate adjustment; shot 01 needs a visible and truthful
product movement; shot 02 keeps lamp position, notebook and hand consistent.
Required resources: working lamp, desk, notebook, performer, camera. The example
does not establish improved illumination or any other unsupplied performance claim.

Reject: one row containing a close-up, a room-wide reveal and three locations,
with 25 spoken words squeezed into two seconds and no timing warning.

## E8 — Target duration and core tradeoff

If a 30-second target cannot plausibly accommodate the approved explanation,
propose which material could be removed and what meaning would be lost. Do not
silently remove the product proof or pretend speech will fit. Until the core cut
is confirmed, keep a marked discussion draft. After one failed quality repair,
export warnings with the affected shots rather than presenting a production-ready
artifact. Surface an unsupported claim as invalid, not as recommended dialogue.

## E9 — Distinct uncertainty categories

“May invite participation” is an analytical hypothesis, not a false observed fact.
“Generated copy claims a nonexistent feature” is a factual defect, not an
experimental direction. “The scene needs four performers” is a resource
requirement, not proof those performers are available. Score these separately.
