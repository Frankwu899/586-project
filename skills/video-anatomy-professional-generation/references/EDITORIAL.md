# Editorial standard: concise, prominent, clean

Make professional judgment easy to understand. Put the main conclusion or creative
proposition first, with a concrete title that expresses the point.
Use the smallest complete explanation. Concision must preserve meaning, useful
specificity, necessary shot detail and material uncertainty.

## Three layers of information
1. **Main result:** the diagnosis, assessable concept or usable storyboard. A reader
   should quickly understand what matters and what decision it supports.
2. **Supporting detail:** evidence links, graph detail, research notes and execution
   requirements. Make them available on demand when the host supports it; in plain
   text, place a short supporting section after the main result. Do not fabricate UI.
3. **Material limitations and decisions:** keep these beside the affected claim,
   direction or shot. Do not bury incomplete evidence or an unresolved core-message
   cut in an appendix.

These are presentation principles, not new JSON fields or permission to hide
required structured data. Never remove source IDs, separate audio/text semantics,
approval references or status from storage to make the screen shorter.

## Editing rules
- State one meaningful point per sentence or card. Lead with the consequence,
  then give the evidence or mechanism that makes it credible.
- Combine overlapping points; delete generic praise, jargon and repeated rationale.
- Give more space to the choices that explain this particular video or task.
  Do not assign every analytical dimension its own card.
- Use concrete verbs: "makes the adjustment visible" is more useful than
  "leverages an engaging demonstration strategy."
- Mark inference locally ("suggests", "may") without making every sentence vague.
  Say "unknown" when there is insufficient support.
- Preserve a supported alternative interpretation without choosing an official
  primary intent. Editorial prominence is not a ranking of uncertain hypotheses.
- Let tables serve comparison or timed execution. Avoid dense tables for a simple
  diagnosis. Do not force a funnel, SWOT, hook/demo/CTA or arbitrary card quota.
- Do not impose an unapproved word limit. Stop when a reader has enough to
  understand, evaluate or execute the result; expand only where needed or requested.

## Final editorial check
Can the reader identify the point immediately? Does each included detail change
understanding or action? Are the evidence basis and uncertainty clear? Could any
sentence be removed without losing something useful? Are all decisive constraints
and warnings still visible?
